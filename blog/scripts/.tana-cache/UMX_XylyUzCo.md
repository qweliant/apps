    - So yeah, I build things. I have always loved a lil project. I love a little show and tell. Doing so means making it usable but forealsies. Data governance is a headache though. Both technically and logistically. For any data that could be considered private or health related you need a legal agreement to store any personal identifiers and that data needs to be placed in a hardened database behind a VPN. Not so easy of a setup for a project I'm the only user of. It seems pertinent to question various data schemes and their implementations as we challenge the existential threat the future of big data operations pose. We seem to move closer to some fucked mix of Parable of the Sower, Ghost in the Shell, and every sci-fi dystopia Arnold Schwarzenegger has been in. As digital artifacts become siloed into various platforms, what those platforms are able to create with the data we store within them has become a prized and sought after resource. Privacy is prized in America but I do not think we have solved where "open data" ends and intellectual property begins.  <!-- node-id: _8I4bMKXoeGI -->
    - Example: are Github's use of code in their platform and the access to internet Archive's digital collections both issues of corporate overreach? They are both adjudicated under Title 17 of the United States Code (Copyright Act). They both concern massive high-stakes data collection program. Github's issue is a fair use concern while The Internet Archive's concerns digital lending. Both are, legally, overreaching programs. Yet, digital archival vs training LLMs feel morally obvious to me. I wonder if this is a human quirk or some blindspot in thinking about IP and copyright.  <!-- node-id: OHTeduoARB4t -->
